\begin{abstract}
Time to a certified solution factorises into an iteration count and a cost per
iteration; the two depend on disjoint parts of a solver recipe. We measure it
on total variation denoising, min-cost flow, multi-commodity flow and optimal
transport, on two GPU architectures. For a fixed instance, certificate cadence
and termination tolerance set the iteration count: at $256$ pixels the
Condat-Vu means $584.0$, $597.3$ and $682.7$ at cadences $16$, $64$ and $256$
agree instance by instance on both devices, and eight execution and kernel
arms agree in 72 of 72 instances, all at cadence $256$, which resolves it only
to the nearest $256$. Run times across them differ by $2.6\times$; the wrong
temporal tile gives $0.778\times$, slower than not fusing. Scheme identity is
inert under XLA ($1.00\times$) but costs $1.30\times$ on a hand written
kernel. Step sizes are worth $4.3\times$ on that count at cadence $256$,
tolerance $10^{-4}$. Fitted on netflow alone, a rule $r = C/\omega_0^p$ in the
scale ratio $\omega_0 = \|c\|/\|b\|$ cuts geometric mean regret against a
per-instance oracle from $2.17\times$ to $1.13\times$ on 162 held-out
instances in three unfitted families, both policies scored on every instance
with capped runs charged at the cap; two estimators bracket $p$ between about
$1.7$ and $2.1$. Two architectures are not a portability claim; the frozen
holdout is unevaluated.
\end{abstract}
