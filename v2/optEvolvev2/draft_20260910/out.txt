A BSTRACT

009
010
011

Solving a convex problem repeatedly on ﬁxed hardware requires choosing
both a mathematical iteration and the way it is executed. We show that
time to a certiﬁed solution factorises as T = N (method, cadence, tolerance) ·
t(execution, fusion, size, device), and that the certiﬁcate cadence is the only recipe
gene appearing in both factors. The factorisation predicts that the two halves can
be tuned separately, which is what we measure, and it names in advance the one
axis on which that separation fails.
We then attack each factor. For the per-iteration cost we localise a factor of two
to a single implementation choice, an inner loop whose trip count is a traced
rather than a static value, and we build a temporally fused CUDA kernel that
advances several primal-dual iterations per launch from shared memory. It is admitted against a sequential reference to 4.6 × 10−15 and is 1.86× faster end to end
on total-variation denoising.
For the iteration count, the step-size ratio is predictable from one measured property of the problem, the ratio of the cost norm to the constraint norm. A rule
ﬁtted on min-cost ﬂow alone, applied unchanged to optimal transport and to multicommodity ﬂow, lands within 1.13× of a per-instance oracle across 162 held-out
instances, against 1.83× for the best single ﬁxed choice.
We measure two GPU architectures, and we report the settings at which each
invariance fails.

Two axes are routinely conﬂated in work on fast ﬁrst-order solvers, and we separate them before
anything else. Arithmetic width is the ﬂoating-point format in which iterates are stored and updated,

597.3 and 682.7 at certiﬁcate cadences K = 16, 64 and 256, certifying 6 of 6. The per-instance

Condat-Vu; under PDHG at K = 16 ﬂoat32 gives 584.0 against 578.7 in ﬂoat64. At 10−6 the same
conﬁguration stops certifying: Condat-Vu solves 0 of 6 and PDHG 1 of 6, against 5 of 6 for ﬂoat64

2.1

GPU FIRST- ORDER SOLVERS FOR LINEAR PROGRAMMING

PDLP established restarted averaged PDHG with presolve, diagonal preconditioning, adaptive step
sizes and a primal weight (Applegate et al., 2021). The accelerator line ports that method to GPUs:
cuPDLP and cuPDLPx (Lu & Yang, 2025; Lu et al., 2025), MPAX as a JAX reimplementation (Lu
et al., 2024), HPR-LP with Halpern iteration and duplicated dual kernels (Chen et al., 2024), PDOT
for optimal transport (Lu & Yang, 2024), and cuOpt as a shipped product (NVIDIA Corporation,
2026). Each reports wall clock for one default conﬁguration and attributes the result to the method.
What is left open is the attribution. On total variation denoising at n = 256 with tolerance 10−4 ,
over a train fold of six scans drawn with data seed 20260907 from a pool of twelve, Condat-Vu
with banked steps needs a mean of 584.0, 597.3 and 682.7 iterations at cadences K = 16, 64 and
256, and the same triple recurs under both arithmetic widths, under both execution structures and
on a second GPU architecture, where the per-instance vectors agree element by element. The hand
written kernel is archived only at K = 64 and K = 256, where it returns 597.3 and 682.7 like the
XLA arm. That invariance is a scope statement and not a law. It covers two GPU architectures,
one problem family, one size and one tolerance, and its width half already fails one size away: at
K = 64 on the second architecture, ﬂoat32 iterates need 586.7 against 576.0 at n = 128 and 1077.3
against 1066.7 at n = 496. The method axis moves that count through its step sizes and not its
name: Condat-Vu and PDHG return byte-identical per-instance counts at K = 256, while balanced
steps cost 4.3125x on iteration count and solve 0 of 6 instances at 10−6 .

whether width is a per-iteration speed knob at all. On one device, at one cadence, at n = 256,
ﬂoat32 buys 1.000x per iteration inside the traced device loop and 1.569x once the loop is chunked,
so the payoff belongs to the loop structure that the width gene happened to select. The ceiling is
also sharper than slower convergence. At n = 256, Condat-Vu with ﬂoat32 iterates and ﬂoat64
certiﬁcation solves 0 of 6 instances at 10−6 , and PDHG solves 1 of 6. On the 4090 that holds at
each of the three archived cadences K = 16, 64 and 256; on the second architecture only K = 64
is archived at this size, and it gives the same 0 of 6 and 1 of 6.

Per-device schedule search is established by Ansor, clSpMV and KernelFoundry (Zheng et al., 2020;
Su & Keutzer, 2012; Wiedemann et al., 2026), and temporal blocking, which fuses several time steps
into one pass over a tile, is standard in stencil computing (Wolf & Lam, 1991). Concurrent work
revisits generated-kernel benchmarking (Zhang et al., 2026). We claim neither as new. What is left

3.4

T HE TEMPORAL FUSION KERNEL

The execution layer contains one construction no catalog gene expresses. The fused kernel runs T
iterations per launch with the tile resident in shared memory. For an output tile of edge B it loads
a shared tile of edge S = B + 2T , a halo of width exactly T on every side. Iteration it computes
the primal on [it + 1, S − it) and the dual and the commit on [it + 1, S − it − 1), so the valid
region shrinks by exactly one cell per side per iteration. After the ﬁnal iteration, at it = T − 1, the
committed region is [T, B + T ), and the write-back reads local index T + o for o ∈ [0, B). The
interval closes exactly, with no slack and no redundant computation. Each fused iteration is three
barrier-separated stages, a primal predict into scratch, a dual clip with an in-place relaxation, and a
primal commit; stages two and three branch on ρ = 1 and write the unblended value, so at ρ = 1
the blend never executes and no extra rounding accumulates.
Shared memory holds ﬁve double arrays over the halo tile, 5(B + 2T )2 · 8 bytes, which caps the
tile size; tiles above 48 KB request the opt-in carve-out. Table 6 gives the measured geometry. Halo
redundancy is S 2 /B 2 and the per-iteration trafﬁc reduction is T divided by it, so the 24 × 4 tile runs
four iterations at 1.7778 times the loads and moves 2.25 times less data per iteration.
Fusion depth is coupled to cadence by a hard divisibility constraint: T must divide K, because the
fused kernel replaces T iterations of the chunk runner and the chunk therefore takes K/T steps.
Measured tiles have T ∈ {2, 3, 4} and measured cadences lie in {64, 128, 256, 512}. This is the
point at which the fusion choice and the certiﬁcate cadence stop being independent. Portability
across the two architectures is by forward JIT of compute_80 PTX rather than by recompilation.
The tile is not currently expressible as a gene. The measurements below drive it from a harness, and
the contract lists no temporal variant among the kernel values. We argue it should become one.

W HERE THE AXES STOP SEPARATING

The structure predicts its own failure modes and the measurements ﬁnd both. An adaptive precision
schedule couples the factors, because the switch iteration is chosen from the gap trajectory, so the
fraction of the run spent in ﬂoat32 depends on the step sizes: the 3.8 percent spread under ﬁxed
precision becomes 16.6 percent under the schedule (Table 19). The two execution decisions also stop
being independent past about 1000 pixels. The static loop’s advantage over the traced bound falls
from 1.673× at 128 pixels through 1.347, 1.291, 1.093 and 1.016 to a minimum of 0.989× at 1488
and 0.993× at 1984, so at the two largest sizes the static bound is mildly harmful rather than merely
exhausted. Fusion over the static loop instead turns U-shaped, at 1.571×, 1.060× and 1.410×
across 128, 992 and 1984 pixels. Each of those is a ratio of summed wall time over the instances
of that sweep rather than a mean of per-instance ratios; the per-instance geometric mean at 128
pixels is 1.580×. The 128-pixel sweep uses six raw crops and the 992 to 1984 pixel sweeps use four
montages each, of two to four distinct scans, since a raw scan caps an honest crop near 496 pixels.
The 1.410× at 1984 pixels is a fusion gain over the static loop at ﬂoat64 throughout, 2.2407709
seconds static over 1.5890337 seconds for the 24 × 4 tile, and is not a precision comparison. The
execution structure and the tile must be chosen jointly at large size and can be chosen independently
at small.

Time to a certiﬁed solution factorises into an iteration count set by the method, the certiﬁcate cadence
and the tolerance, and a cost per iteration set by the hardware, the execution structure, the kernel and
the size. At ﬁxed size and ﬁxed cadence the ﬁrst factor is invariant to the hardware, the execution
structure and the kernel, exactly, in 72 of 72 measured instances across two architectures and seven
sizes. It is not invariant to size: at K = 64 in ﬂoat64 with condat_vu_banked, N is 576.000 at
n=128, 597.333 at n=256, 736.000 at n=384 and 1066.667 at n=496.
Cadence is the gene the two factors share within the recipe, and it carries the largest single variance
share in cost per iteration. It is not the only gene in both. Size appears in both factors as well, and
precision changes N at some sizes: on the Blackwell at K = 64, f32_cert64 gives 586.667
against ﬂoat64’s 576.000 at n=128 and 1077.333 against 1066.667 at n=496, while at n=256 the
two agree at 597.333.
Three consequences follow. Kernel search runs against a ﬁxed arithmetic budget: on the two architectures measured, the iteration count was identical at every size run on both, so N can be held
ﬁxed within that scope while the second factor is timed. Legality is decided symbolically before
any solve, by a gate that is an exact function of θ and family dependent in its threshold, veriﬁed as
sufﬁcient for admission and never as necessary; it admits on TV what it rejects on netﬂow, where the
PDHG gate product τ σkLk2 runs 1.02267, 1.03040, 1.03577 and 1.03781 at sizes 12, 16, 24 and
32. Every rejected row is a balanced conﬁguration, 24 of 24, and no banked conﬁguration is ever
rejected. And the recipe is predictable from measured structure where a ﬁxed choice is not: scaling
the step ratio as C/ω02 with C = 0.977 ﬁtted on netﬂow alone cuts pooled geomean regret on 162
held-out instances from 2.168× to 1.131× and lifts coverage from 146 to 162 of 162, at an exponent
consistent with, though not pinned to, the value 2 implied by PDLP’s primal weight.
The numerical admission run for the temporal fusion kernel is archived with the other artifacts: 106
lines holding 102 checked cases, 17 tile and fused-depth variants at three image shapes including a
non-square 100 by 130, in both proximal settings. The worst absolute deviation against sequential
single-step reference launches is 4.649 × 10−15 and the verdict is PASS within ﬂoat64 tolerance. No
case is bitwise identical, so the admission is numerical and not a ﬂoating-point equivalence proof.

AVERAGED BLENDING

The extended genome can represent avg(λ; T1 , T2 ) = λT1 +(1−λ)T2 . For averaged maps in a common metric with a nonempty common ﬁxed-point set, Lemma 3 gives the blended certiﬁcate (Ryu
& Yin, 2022; Combettes & Yamada, 2015). The current gate admits restricted cases: compatible
FBS parents can use different steps, DRS/Davis–Yin parents must preserve the auxiliary ﬁxed-point
encoding through identical steps, and saddle parents require identical (τ, σ) so their metric and saddle encoding agree. The common ﬁxed-point condition remains a semantic mathematical obligation;
the checker is not a general decision procedure for that property.
Blending lies outside the grammar of Theorem 1 and is not used in the hardware factorial. In the
standard LP saddle instantiation, PDHG and Condat–Vũ with identical parameters are the same
map, so blending those parents adds no mathematical variation. The expression contains two parent
evaluations, but actual execution cost depends on compiler elimination and fusion and must be
measured. We make no empirical discovery claim for this extension in the current evaluation.
