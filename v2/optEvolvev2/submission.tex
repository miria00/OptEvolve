\begin{abstract}
OptEvolve searches hardware-aware executable recipes for convex optimization:
a mathematical splitting, its parameters, and the precision, kernel and
certificate-checking schedule used to execute it. A construction gate checks
sufficient convergence conditions before execution, while a separate numerical
check evaluates returned solutions in the original problem coordinates.
In a development catalog of 36 total-variation solver recipes, execution-policy
selection reduces warm-time scores by factors of $1.47$ and $1.57$ on an
RTX 4090 and RTX 5090, respectively, relative to a compiled float64 baseline
checking every 64 iterations. All six validation OCT images reach $10^{-4}$.
Both GPUs select float32-to-float64 refinement with a longer check interval,
while CPU selection retains the baseline. Joint selection matches
implementation-first sequential selection on both GPUs. At $10^{-6}$, the
selected recipes solve five of six images under the iteration cap; pure
float32 iterations can fail despite float64 checking. A separate LP
diagnostic finds that the tested generated CUDA kernels are slower than XLA
despite fewer launches. These scoped findings motivate hardware-aware
evaluation but do not establish a joint-search or LLM-guidance advantage.
Mathematical guarantees assume exact arithmetic and correct atoms; numerical
tests are not formal floating-point proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
The compiler instantiations and reference comparisons are described in
Appendix~\ref{app:instantiation}. Hardware campaign artifacts record serialized
genomes, source and input hashes, selection partitions, compiler/device
versions, cold and warm timings, individual numerical checks and non-solves.
The current evaluation is diagnostic; the frozen final benchmark is not yet
reported. The supplementary package remains to be assembled for submission.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
The imaging experiments solve a convex denoising objective on an existing OCT
dataset. Optimization accuracy does not establish preservation of pathology
or suitability for clinical decisions. We report no clinical efficacy result.
Dataset attribution and applicable redistribution terms must accompany the
submission artifact; the code should load data through its documented source.

\section*{AI Use Statement}
Language models are part of the proposal method and have also assisted the
research workflow, including literature discovery, experiment planning, code
implementation, debugging, manuscript drafting and interpretation of results.
The local proposal pilot uses Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct. Coding assistants,
including Claude and Codex, contributed to implementation and writing.
The authors are responsible for verifying the mathematics, citations, code
and reported measurements. A complete model/version and usage inventory will
accompany the final submission disclosure.
