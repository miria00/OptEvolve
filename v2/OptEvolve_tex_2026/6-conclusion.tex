\section{Conclusion}

OptEvolve searches reusable convex-solver designs and routes instances by
measured time to an independently accepted answer on target hardware. Its
pre-execution gate checks sufficient convergence conditions for exact splitting
iterations; finite tests screen generated numerical code; and an
original-problem check accepts returned points. These checks have different
scopes.

On budget-constrained least squares at $n=48{,}000$, where no packaged
specialist exists, formulation relocation and a synthesized projection produce
a $25.22\times$ faster cold solve at $10^{-4}$ than the fastest accepted engine
at shipped defaults. An ablation at $n=12{,}000$ shows that formulation
accounts for most of this family's gain. Across the
held-out field, routing solves $27/28$ instances within budget versus $26/28$
for a best-per-instance oracle limited to existing competitor engines. In
three of six application classes, a baseline returns a point that fails the common
accuracy check; GPU tile choices also differ by device. These results support
joint mathematical and hardware-aware solver search for reusable workloads,
while specialists win in other regimes and first-visit exploration remains
costly.
