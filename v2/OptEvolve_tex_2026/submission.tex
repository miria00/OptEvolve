\begin{abstract}
Choosing a convex solver couples mathematical validity with hardware cost:
changing a formulation changes both the iteration and which implementation is
fastest. We introduce \textbf{OptEvolve}, in which an LLM proposes typed
splitting schemes, solution-preserving formulations, projection implementations, and
hardware backends for a problem family. Before a proposed scheme is timed, a
symbolic gate checks sufficient convergence conditions for its \emph{exact}
iteration. Generated projections and kernels pass separate finite numerical
tests, and every returned point faces an independent accuracy check on the
original problem. Search and routing use measured time to an accepted answer
on the target device. On budget-constrained least squares at $n=48{,}000$,
where no packaged specialist exists, a searched formulation and projection
give a $25.22\times$ faster cold solve at tolerance $10^{-4}$ than the fastest
accepted engine at shipped defaults. A separate $n=12{,}000$ ablation shows
that formulation accounts for most of this family's gain. Across $28$
held-out QP, LP, and SOCP instances, routing solves $27$
within budget versus $26$ for a best-per-instance oracle restricted to
existing competitor engines, although first-visit exploration is costly. In a
six-size OCT kernel sweep, device-specific CUDA fusion improves iteration-loop
time by up to $2.74\times$ over XLA, with the best tile differing at five sizes
between an RTX~4090 and H100; this is a kernel-loop, not a full-solve, gain.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
The anonymized code, reproduction scripts, solver genomes and certificates,
configurations, seeds, and data manifests are available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/OptEvolve-8104/README.md}.
Public data sources and evaluation conventions are documented in the companion
experiment log (Sections~\ref{LOG-app:log:data} and~\ref{LOG-app:log:conventions}).
Archived genomes allow rerunning the reported solvers without regenerating
them; an independent LLM search may produce different proposals.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
We followed the ICLR Code of Ethics. No new patient data or human-participant
experiments were collected. Retinal OCT images come from a previously released
dataset with randomized patient identifiers; we measure denoising computation,
not diagnostic accuracy. A solver's convergence certificate does not establish
clinical safety; consequential uses require independent application-specific
validation.
