\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{LLM-driven algorithm search.}
FunSearch uses generated programs and evaluator feedback to find mathematical constructions; AlphaEvolve extends program search to more general algorithmic and computational tasks \citep{RomeraParedes2023Mathematical,Novikov2025AlphaEvolve}. OpenEvolve supplies an open implementation with population diversity; ShinkaEvolve targets sample-efficient proposal selection; GEPA feeds diagnostic traces back to mutation; AdaEvolve allocates search resources dynamically; and EvoX evolves search strategies alongside solutions \citep{Sharma2025OpenEvolve,Lange2025ShinkaEvolve,Agrawal2025GEPA,Cemri2026AdaEvolve,Liu2026EvoX}. SkyDiscover provides modular search infrastructure used here for projection synthesis \citep{Liu2026SkyDiscover}. These works improve how programs are proposed and evaluated. Our complementary constraint is \emph{what} a solver proposal must satisfy before evaluation: typed mathematical schemes pass a convergence gate, whereas generated numerical projections pass a separate contract harness. Section~\ref{sec:exp:llm} compares with LLM-only operator evolution.

\paragraph{Mathematical checking.}
IMO-Bench distinguishes proof assessment from short-answer grading; AlphaGeometry couples generation with symbolic deduction; DeepSeekMath rewards verifiable answers \citep{Luong2025Towards,Trinh2024Solving,Shao2024DeepSeekMath}. They motivate checking rather than trusting LLM output, but do not certify an optimization update. Proof-assistant approaches such as CvxLean and broader deductive-verification work address a stronger, different goal \citep{Bentkamp2023VerifiedReductions,Brugger2025Toward}. OptEvolve's construction certificates check sufficient mathematical side conditions, not the generated floating-point implementation or a machine-checked proof of code.

\paragraph{Solver design and selection.}
Generic engines in the comparison field include Clarabel, PIQP, OSQP, SCS, ECOS, and HiGHS \citep{Goulart2024Clarabel,Schwan2023PIQP,Stellato2020OSQP,ODonoghue2016Conic,Domahidi2013ECOS,Huangfu2017Parallelizing,HiGHS2026Solvers}. PDLP and HPR-LP combine splitting with preconditioning, adaptive parameters, or restarts \citep{Applegate2021Practical,Chen2024HPRLP}; RLQP learns penalty updates for a QP solver \citep{Ichnowski2021Accelerating}. Their adaptive policies are not certified by our gate. Solver selection itself has precedents: Hydra configures complementary portfolio members, and formulation selection has been learned for mixed-integer quadratic programming \citep{Xu2010Hydra,Bonami2022Classifier}. OptEvolve differs by expanding a portfolio across scheme parameters, solution-preserving formulations, tested projection implementations, and hardware choices, then pricing evolved candidates against existing engines.

\paragraph{Strong structured baselines.}
Generic-solver comparisons alone miss coordinate descent and working-set methods for lasso and elastic net \citep{Friedman2010Regularization,Pedregosa2011Scikit,Massias2018Celer,Bertrand2022Beyond}, LIBLINEAR and LIBSVM for SVMs \citep{Fan2008LIBLINEAR,Chang2011LIBSVM}, L-BFGS-B for smooth bounded problems \citep{Byrd1995Limited}, network simplex for transport \citep{Bonneel2011Displacement,Flamary2021POT}, and Frank--Wolfe core-set methods for the minimum enclosing ball \citep{Yildirim2008Two,LacosteJulien2015Global}. Box--hyperplane projection also has a classical breakpoint algorithm \citep{Helgason1980Polynomially,Kiwiel2008Breakpoint}. These specialists join the field when applicable. Comparisons use a common acceptance check on the original problem, and competitors run at their shipped defaults, which addresses the weak-baseline problem identified by \citet{McGreivy2024Weak} without tuning a competitor on its behalf. Beyond generated families, we use established LP, QP, and conic benchmarks, including Netlib and MIPLIB LP relaxations \citep{Netlib2026Linear,Gleixner2021MIPLIB}. Benchopt is the closest harness to ours: it automates cross-language, cross-hardware optimization benchmarks and scores solvers by suboptimality against the best objective observed in the run \citep{Moreau2022Benchopt}. OptEvolve-Bench differs in what counts as a result. Acceptance is an independently recomputed certificate on the original problem, so it does not depend on the field that happened to be run, the comparison field must contain the structure-specific specialist, and the finest certifiable tolerance is reported next to time. CUTEst and MIPLIB-NL are related nonlinear and language-specified testbeds, respectively, but are not evaluated here \citep{Gould2014CUTEst,Li2026Constructing}.
