\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{LLM-driven algorithm search.}
FunSearch and AlphaEvolve search programs using evaluator feedback \citep{RomeraParedes2023Mathematical,Novikov2025AlphaEvolve}. ReEvo already combines population-based evolution, LLM reflection, and execution feedback for combinatorial-optimization heuristics \citep{ye2024reevo}; evaluator feedback inside an evolutionary loop is therefore not our contribution. OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, GEPA, AdaEvolve, and EvoX vary the population or proposal policy \citep{Sharma2025OpenEvolve,Lange2025ShinkaEvolve,Agrawal2025GEPA,Cemri2026AdaEvolve,Liu2026EvoX}. SkyDiscover supplies infrastructure used here for projection synthesis \citep{Liu2026SkyDiscover}. OptEvolve instead restricts which exact solver schemes may run: a mathematical gate checks sufficient convergence conditions before evaluation, while generated numerical code receives separate tests. The fixed-budget resampling and random controls in Appendix~\ref{app:llm-loop} probe feedback inside this gated space; the random-proposer result does not show an LLM advantage.

\paragraph{Mathematical checking.}
AutoOPT is the closest optimization-discovery comparison: numerical BnB--PEP design precedes LLM derivation of analytic methods and proofs, Lean verification, and human interpretation \citep{Kim2026AutoOPT}. Its formal proof checks are stronger than ours, while its staged proof workflow and fixed-step smooth-minimization objective differ from a pre-execution gate inside a population loop priced by time to accepted answers on target hardware. Proof-assistant approaches such as CvxLean likewise address a stronger verification goal \citep{Bentkamp2023VerifiedReductions,Brugger2025Toward}. OptEvolve checks sufficient mathematical side conditions for exact splitting iterations; it does not machine-check generated floating-point code. Hawkeye uses architecture-specific tests for generated GPU kernels \citep{Hawkeye2026}; our measured backend choices also affect solver formulation and routing.

\paragraph{Solver design and selection.}
Generic engines in the comparison field include Clarabel, PIQP, OSQP, SCS, ECOS, and HiGHS \citep{Goulart2024Clarabel,Schwan2023PIQP,Stellato2020OSQP,ODonoghue2016Conic,Domahidi2013ECOS,Huangfu2017Parallelizing,HiGHS2026Solvers}. PDLP and HPR-LP combine splitting with preconditioning, adaptive parameters, or restarts \citep{Applegate2021Practical,Chen2024HPRLP}; RLQP learns penalty updates for a QP solver \citep{Ichnowski2021Accelerating}. Their adaptive policies are not certified by our gate. Solver selection itself has precedents: Hydra configures complementary portfolio members, and formulation selection has been learned for mixed-integer quadratic programming \citep{Xu2010Hydra,Bonami2022Classifier}. OptEvolve differs by expanding a portfolio across scheme parameters, solution-preserving formulations, tested projection implementations, and hardware choices, then pricing evolved candidates against existing engines.

\paragraph{Strong structured baselines.}
Generic-solver comparisons alone miss coordinate descent and working-set methods for lasso and elastic net \citep{Friedman2010Regularization,Pedregosa2011Scikit,Massias2018Celer,Bertrand2022Beyond}, LIBLINEAR and LIBSVM for SVMs \citep{Fan2008LIBLINEAR,Chang2011LIBSVM}, L-BFGS-B for smooth bounded problems \citep{Byrd1995Limited}, network simplex for transport \citep{Bonneel2011Displacement,Flamary2021POT}, and Frank--Wolfe core-set methods for the minimum enclosing ball \citep{Yildirim2008Two,LacosteJulien2015Global}. Box--hyperplane projection also has a classical breakpoint algorithm \citep{Helgason1980Polynomially,Kiwiel2008Breakpoint}. These specialists join the field when applicable. Comparisons use a common original-problem acceptance check, and competitors run at their shipped defaults; this measures out-of-the-box performance at a target accuracy, not the best performance after competitor tuning \citep{McGreivy2024Weak}. Beyond generated families, we use established LP, QP, and conic benchmarks, including Netlib and MIPLIB LP relaxations \citep{Netlib2026Linear,Gleixner2021MIPLIB}. Benchopt already provides reproducible cross-language, cross-hardware optimization benchmarks \citep{Moreau2022Benchopt}. OptEvolve-Bench adds a fixed original-problem acceptance check, a comparison field with recognized specialists, and the finest accepted tolerance beside time. When the reference optimum is numerical rather than a proven bound, this is an empirical acceptance check, not a formal optimality certificate. CUTEst and MIPLIB-NL are related nonlinear and language-specified testbeds, respectively, but are not evaluated here \citep{Gould2014CUTEst,Li2026Constructing}.
